PanAX: Accelerating Epidemic Science through Causally-Informed Domain Generalization and Knowledge Transfer

PanAX develops causally informed, data-driven methods for rapidly adapting epidemic models to emerging outbreak contexts. The project focuses on modeling, forecasting, uncertainty, causal explanation, knowledge transfer, computational discovery, and decision support when observations are incomplete, delayed, or noisy.

Significant results

Research Results

R1

Modular epidemic simulation platform

Modeling, forecasting, and uncertainty

PanAX integrates transmission, testing, immunity, hospitalization, mobility, and interventions while continuously learning epidemiological parameters from observed data using sequential Monte Carlo techniques. The framework supports intervention analysis, outbreak detection, and policy evaluation.

R2

Comparative forecasting across diseases and surveillance systems

Modeling, forecasting, and uncertainty

Comparative evaluations of mechanistic, phenomenological, statistical, machine-learning, and hybrid forecasting approaches show that model performance depends on epidemic phase and forecasting horizon. The work provides practical guidance for model selection.

R3

Forecasting from wastewater surveillance

Modeling, forecasting, and uncertainty

PanAX evaluates eleven forecasting approaches across 133 sequential forecasts using national and regional COVID-19 wastewater surveillance data. Ensemble sub-epidemic models perform best at three- and four-week horizons, while ARIMA and generalized additive models perform well at shorter horizons.

R4

Adaptive ensemble forecasting

Modeling, forecasting, and uncertainty

The project develops adaptive ensemble forecasting methods based on multi-armed bandit algorithms that adjust model weights according to recent performance. These methods improve probabilistic forecasting relative to static ensemble weighting schemes.

R5

Hybrid mechanistic and deep-learning forecasts

Modeling, forecasting, and uncertainty

Hybrid frameworks combine mechanistic epidemic models with Monte Carlo Dropout LSTM architectures. Across multiple influenza-like illness waves, the ensembles outperform individual component models on forecasting accuracy and uncertainty metrics.

R6

Inference, identifiability, and uncertainty

Causal learning, explainability, and knowledge transfer

Controlled comparisons of Bayesian and frequentist inference methods show that frequentist approaches perform well under full observability, while Bayesian approaches provide stronger uncertainty characterization under partial observability. Identifiability strongly influences forecast reliability.

R7

Neural simulation-based inference

Causal learning, explainability, and knowledge transfer

Comparisons of approximate Bayesian computation and neural simulation-based inference show that neural posterior estimation produces more accurate posterior distributions and improved predictive performance under fixed simulation budgets.

R8

Domain disentanglement for data-scarce forecasting

Causal learning, explainability, and knowledge transfer

A domain-disentanglement framework separates location-specific epidemic dynamics from transferable epidemic behavior, selectively transferring knowledge from data-rich communities to data-poor communities. The largest gains occur with limited historical observations and long forecasting horizons.

R9

Benchmarking infrastructure for causal machine learning

Causal learning, explainability, and knowledge transfer

PanAX develops benchmarking infrastructure for causal discovery, causal effect estimation, and causal inference. The platform supports reproducible comparison across datasets, metrics, computational environments, and deployment settings.

R10

Explainable multivariate time-series reasoning

Causal learning, explainability, and knowledge transfer

The project combines simulation-generated counterfactuals, symbolic rule representations, and large language models to derive interpretable explanations from multivariate time-series data. Resulting rules generalize to unseen trajectories and describe delayed causal relationships.

R11

Causally informed skyline search

Causal learning, explainability, and knowledge transfer

A causally informed skyline framework selectively removes correlations that interfere with efficient skyline discovery while preserving correlations aligned with user preferences. Experiments show fewer dominance checks and lower execution time.

R12

Causal situational awareness

Causal learning, explainability, and knowledge transfer

PanAX integrates forecasting, causal reasoning, uncertainty quantification, and explanation into a unified decision-support paradigm. This shifts epidemic support systems from prediction alone toward intervention planning.

R13

Gradient-guided parameter-space exploration

Computational discovery, optimization, and decision support

A gradient-guided parameter-space exploration framework allocates evaluations to regions where system behavior changes most rapidly. Compared with Latin hypercube sampling and Bayesian optimization approaches, it improves characterization under fixed evaluation budgets.

R14

Systems framework for built-environment microbiomes

Computational discovery, optimization, and decision support

The project connects microbial ecology, environmental controls, occupancy patterns, ventilation systems, and architectural design choices within a common modeling perspective, identifying drivers of microbial persistence and transmission in engineered environments.

Research Focus

PanAX connects epidemic simulation, forecasting, causal reasoning, and scalable data management so models and evidence from richer settings inform data-scarce outbreak contexts.

Models Complex dynamic epidemic systems with mobility, behavior, testing, immunity, and interventions.
Causality Explanations, counterfactuals, and causal structure for interpretable decisions.
Transfer Domain-generalization methods for data-rich to data-poor settings.
Tools Open-source software for researchers, epidemiologists, and public-health practitioners.
Research

Research agenda

PanAX advances three connected research themes: modeling and forecasting under uncertainty, causal learning and knowledge transfer, and computational discovery for decision support.

Theme Forecasting

Modeling, Forecasting, and Uncertainty

PanAX develops spatio-temporal epidemic models, forecasting methods, and uncertainty quantification techniques that remain effective under incomplete, delayed, or noisy observations.

Theme Causal transfer

Causal Learning and Knowledge Transfer

PanAX distinguishes context-specific epidemic behavior from mechanisms that remain stable across outbreaks, regions, and populations.

Theme Optimization

Computational Discovery and Decision Support

PanAX develops scalable methods for model reuse, parameter-space exploration, informative scenario discovery, and intervention analysis under resource constraints.

Activities

Ongoing project activities

Dynamic epidemic simulation

The project develops computational frameworks for modeling and simulation of complex epidemic systems with transmission, testing, immunity, hospitalization, mobility, and intervention effects.

Inference and uncertainty

The project develops methods for parameter estimation, uncertainty quantification, and identifiability analysis using Bayesian, frequentist, and simulation-based inference techniques.

Domain generalization

The project develops domain-generalization and knowledge-transfer methods that separate domain-invariant and domain-specific information for data-scarce settings.

Causal situational awareness

The project integrates forecasting, causal reasoning, uncertainty quantification, and explanation to support planning and intervention in complex dynamic systems.
